\section{Simulator and Experimental Configuration}
\label{app:sim_fidelity}

This appendix supplies the detailed configuration omitted from Section~\ref{sec:experiments}: the simulator-versus-real-GPU validation, the full parameter table for each workload, and the precise hyperparameter settings of each scheduler.

\subsection{Simulator validation against real GPU}

The numerical experiments of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} are conducted on the Vidur discrete-event simulator. To verify that the simulator's iteration-time predictions match real GPU behavior in the regime relevant to those experiments, we compare Vidur's predictions against direct measurements taken on a physical NVIDIA A100~80\,GB GPU running Llama-2-7B served by vLLM. We evaluate $25$ batch-size configurations $B \in \{1, 2, 4, \ldots, 128\}$ at fixed prefill length $256$ and decode length $20$, which together span the batch-size range used in our experiments.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Experiments_pdf/figure_A_sim_fidelity.pdf}
    \caption{Validation of the Vidur simulator against real NVIDIA A100~80\,GB GPU measurements. Each point is one batch-size configuration $B \in \{1, 2, 4, \ldots, 128\}$ ($N = 25$), with prefill $256$ and decode $20$, serving Llama-2-7B via vLLM. The dashed line is $y = x$. The linear iteration-time model of Equation~\eqref{eq:time_consump} achieves $R^2 = 0.9957$ and mean absolute percentage error $1.51\%$, confirming the simulator's fidelity for the regime relevant to our experiments.}
    \label{fig:sim_fidelity}
\end{figure}

As Figure~\ref{fig:sim_fidelity} shows, Vidur's predictions track the GPU measurements to within mean absolute percentage error $1.51\%$ ($R^2 = 0.9957$), which justifies using the simulator as the basis for our throughput and stability comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The fitted iteration-time intercept and slope are $d_0 \approx 276$\,ms and $d_1 \approx 0.0115$\,ms per KV-cache token, in line with the linear time model of Equation~\eqref{eq:time_consump}.

\subsection{Scheduler hyperparameters}

\textbf{vLLM.} We use vLLM's default scheduler, which serves new arrivals first under FCFS. The KV-cache pool is configured with the default watermark fraction $0.01$ (i.e.\ $1\%$ of the pool is reserved as a safety margin to absorb growth from in-flight decodes).

\textbf{Sarathi.} We use chunked prefill with chunk size $512$ tokens per iteration and an admission cap of $512$ requests. Other settings follow the defaults of \citet{agrawal2023sarathi}.

\textbf{WAIT.} We use chunk size $256$ and total threshold $\sum_k n_k = 21$. The same configuration is used at every $\lambda$ in the single-type sweep.

\textbf{Nested WAIT.} We use chunk size $256$ and total threshold $\sum_k n_k = 22$ on the multi-type W3 workload. On the real lmsys workload we tune $(\mathrm{cs}, \sum_k n_k)$ per QPS to minimize mean latency, drawing from $\mathrm{cs} \in \{96, 128, 192, 256, 384\}$ and $\sum_k n_k \in \{20, 26, 30, 35, 40\}$; the tuned configuration at each QPS is recorded in our open-sourced experiment artifacts.
